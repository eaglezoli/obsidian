### **1. Clarity and focus of the research question(s)** – 15%  

- Broad topic or area of study (keep non-technical)  
- Specific research issue or problem to address  
- Optionally: one main question + one or two subsidiary questions  
- Academic basis and justification for the question  
- Outline of key education issues related to the question  
- Why is the topic of general importance and relevant to you?  
- Scope and boundaries of the study  
- Research objectives (aim for five)  
  - Examples:  
    - Critical review of relevant literature  
    - Overview of study context  
    - Detailed case study  
    - Identify implications for policy/practice  

---

### **2. Critical discussion and synthesis of the literature** – 25%  

- Search strategy:  
  - Databases used  
  - Keywords  
  - Years of literature searched  
  - Countries included in search  
- Preliminary literature review of key sources supporting the need for the study  

---

### **3. Clarity and appropriateness of methodology & ethical issues** – 30% 

- Appropriate research methodology and rationale (e.g., phenomenology, evaluation, exploratory)  
- Sample derivation and participant recruitment  
- Data collection and analysis methods  
- Ethical approval requirements  
- Ethical principles considered (confidentiality, anonymity, informed consent, insider research)  
- Timetable for research activities  

---

### **4. Development, structure, and referencing** – 10%  

- Clear, concise, and logical structure  
- Accurate referencing using a recognised system (Harvard preferred)  
- Avoid footnotes/endnotes if possible (may affect word count)  
- Allocate time for editing and proofreading before submission
